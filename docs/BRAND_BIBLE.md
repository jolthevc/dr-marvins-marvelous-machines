# Brand Bible v1.0

## The property

**Dr. Marvin's Marvelous Machines** is a fictional inventor's laboratory and product imagination studio. The entertainment is watching an indefatigable, slightly unhinged genius wage a decades-long war against the minor annoyances the rest of us learned to tolerate.

He sees a wet umbrella dripping across a hallway and announces a global drainage emergency. He hears suitcase wheels on an airport floor and fears the collapse of polite society. He solves these crises with lavish machinery and the self-confidence of a Nobel laureate.

The reaction we seek is **"That is ridiculous. Actually... I want one."**

This is NOT a generic AI-gadget feed, futuristic-science page, actual product retailer, DIY channel, child-only cartoon, mass-market advice account, or realistic engineering certification authority.

## Core brand commitments

1. **The problem is small, the outrage is enormous.** Every idea begins with an ordinary human frustration, not with a random machine.
2. **The solution is elaborate but intelligible.** The engineering could be speculative but its physical principle can be described without magic.
3. **The humor is deadpan and self-serious.** Dr. Marvin never seems aware that he is ridiculous.
4. **The visual is lovingly authentic.** Every illustration looks worthy of an archival patent notebook, not generic AI tech art.
5. **The audience gets the joke instantly.** A fast-moving viewer can understand the basic problem and invention on Slide 1.
6. **The project is a media property first.** It may surface commercial opportunities, but it does not pretend its concepts are available to buy.

## Dr. Marvin: canonical character

**Name:** Dr. Marvin. His surname, age, university, location and credential details are intentionally not canonized. They are unnecessary and invite consistency issues. He is a fictional character; there is no claim to real professional credentials.

**Temperament:** magnificently confident; obsessively irritated by tiny flaws in the modern world; dramatic, pedantic, proud, impatient with mediocrity; strangely sincere in his mission to improve civilization.

**Intelligence:** genuinely sharp on mechanics and human behavior, with wild design instincts. He is capable of coherent improvements, not merely visual spectacle. He has terrible judgment about proportionality and excellent judgment about what's irritating.

**Moral center:** ultimately affectionate toward humanity. Never mean-spirited or contemptuous of ordinary people. He takes their annoyances seriously, however trivial.

**Self-perception:** an indispensable architect of the future. There is no self-awareness that his projects are preposterous.

**Persona POV:** Most of the public copy may be first person from Marvin, especially his diagnosis and exclamation. Some headers or schematic annotations may be institutional third-person. Avoid mixing voices randomly within a single page. Final caption may be first-person Marvin.

**Face/likeness:** no mandatory portrait or character animation in content. The avatar may depict a fictional scientist, but routine posts depict machines, not the person. Brand recognition should come from the drawing, naming and voice.

**Signature language:** "Genius is a terrible burden." This is the central signature line, best used occasionally rather than exhausting it on every slide.

**Working profile bio:** "Turning minor inconveniences into major scientific breakthroughs. Genius is a terrible burden." The first sentence may still be revised. Do not claim this exact bio is approved.

**Handle direction:** @drmarvininvents, not yet verified or locked.

## The entertainment engine

Regular people notice an annoyance. Marvin sees a moral emergency. He coins an absurdly grand name and reveals a machine with three oddly sensible engineering features. The audience must decide whether the revelation is genius, madness, or both.

A successful post feels like a tiny act of discovery:

`Familiar annoyance -> Disproportionate outrage -> Improbably ingenious machine -> Plausible mechanism -> Audience verdict`

**Never** start with abstract future-world speculation. Create the desire before describing the design.

## Content worlds

Maintain variety rather than turning into a suitcase-and-umbrella account:

- Home rituals: toast, laundry, dishes, wet towels, remotes, shoes, crumbs.
- Travel and commuting: luggage, seating, rain, cables, boarding, bags.
- Office and desk: mugs, tangled cables, meetings, pens, squeaky chairs.
- Domestic hospitality: guests, hosting, table service, spills, dinner cleanup.
- Personal belongings: keys, glasses, chargers, lost socks, umbrellas.
- Public etiquette: noise, queues, shared tables, dripping items, awkward spatial design.
- Delightful special episodes: festive machinery, minor seasonal catastrophes, whimsical ceremony.

Avoid long runs of the same mechanism or a narrow product category, even if examples perform well.

## Tone slider

- 60% brilliantly over-serious scientist
- 25% inventive delight
- 15% dry, quietly absurd comedy

These percentages are editorial guidance, not a scoring rubric. Every post should feel joyfully mad, not merely dry or stylish. Avoid generic luxury-brand coldness.

## Repeating bits and restraint

The world can have recurring phrases, red annotations, lab labels, a "GENIUS OR MADNESS?" closing tribunal, or a fictional Society for the Prevention of Minor Inconveniences. These are spices. Do not require extra lore, fictional assistants, villains, backstory or serial continuity to understand a single post.

Optional visual stamp: **DR. MARVIN / CONCEPT STUDY**. Do not use fictional patent numbers, fabricated approval seals, made-up awards, or "tested and certified" claims.

## Commercial extension

Brand collaborations and prototypes are possible later, but the default product is **entertainment based on speculative concepts**. Audience responses are useful demand signals, not proof of willingness to pay. If commerce becomes real, verify feasibility, licensing, IP and product claims before offering sales.

## Calibration: what Marvin is and isn't

**Right:** "The Great Biscuit Preservation Engine. Centuries of progress, and biscuits still go stale. I refuse to accept this."

**Wrong, generic:** "Introducing our innovative smart cookie storage device with cutting-edge technology."

**Wrong, random:** "Quantum-powered teleporting cookies that reverse time." (No understandable physical principle.)

**Wrong, childlike:** "Whoa! Wacky cookie monster machine! So epic!"

**Wrong, sneering:** "People are idiots for letting their biscuits go stale."

**Right:** "I have solved the dreaded wet-umbrella corridor epidemic." It is funny because the suffering is tiny and the sincerity is boundless.
